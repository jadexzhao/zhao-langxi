\noindent\textit{Note · Hong Kong · not peer-reviewed}

Looking human is not the product. I am Digital Humans Project Lead at Luddy (Indiana University).
If you are the person who might talk to the face, the test is simple: do you trust it, can you tell what it is, and would you come back.

\section*{What you meet}

Most of these faces are not a new friend. They sit on an enquiry desk: a multilingual kiosk, a customer-service screen, a talking layer over a knowledge base.
You ask a question. You need a clear answer. You need to know you are not talking to a person.
That is the consumer day, not a sales pitch and not a white paper.

\section*{Hong Kong catalogues as evidence}

Hong Kong's public catalogues already list digital humans as customer-service and enquiry tools, including Cantonese, Mandarin, and English.
That is B2C service in public view. It is not a job I held in Hong Kong, and it is not a product I am selling.

The Smart Government Innovation LAB (Cyberport) and the Hong Kong Productivity Council's Digital DIY portal publish those listings. A few public links:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Smart LAB · AI Digital human customer service (China Mobile Hong Kong):
    \url{https://www.smartlab.gov.hk/en/ai_solutions/a-0073}
  \item Smart LAB · Tencent Cloud Digital Human:
    \url{https://www.smartlab.gov.hk/en/ai_solutions/a-0077}
  \item HKPC Digital DIY · DYXnet Digital Human Platform:
    \url{https://ddiy.hkpc.org/en/node/1519}
  \item DYXnet product page (Hong Kong):
    \url{https://www.dyxnet.com/hk/digital-human-platform/}
\end{itemize}

Honest read: a lot of what you see is still video, anchors, and service-hall Q\&A with a face on top.
Useful for ordinary enquiries. Still short of the harder work on trust, disclosure, and coming back.

Working checklist for the Luddy build (Jade's framework only \ldots{} not a published law and not a measured result): Ordinary-Day Fit needs disclosure before the first answer, a human handoff, answers checkable against an owned source, and a knowledge base staff can edit after the demo.
New sources that change the argument: Vesala et al.\ (ACM 2026) place disclosure at the latest at first interaction; PCPD's 2024 Model Framework asks for prominent AI disclosure and human-intervention channels; mainland Deep Synthesis rules require prominent labelling when dialogue or face services may confuse the public.
A face on a knowledge base is not automatic transparency. Looking human is not the product.

Two surfaces opened 29 September 2026 (Jade's observation only): Smart LAB A-0073 scored disclosure / handoff / checkable source / maintainability all not shown; the DYXnet Digital Human product page scored handoff pass (Contact Us) and the other three not shown.
When the face is only a listing or a video, ordinary-day transparency is not yet evidenced. Not a user study.
