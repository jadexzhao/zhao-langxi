A personal AI concierge · research in public

Less scrolling. Better human possibilities.

The AI can do the first pass. You keep the final say.

I am a rising senior studying whether a private AI concierge could understand what matters to you, do the exhausting first pass with other systems, and return only a few human possibilities worth your time. The point is not to automate the relationship. It is to make more room for the right one.

I am not presenting a finished company, a launched dating product, or a proven matching result. I am building the research habit around a hard product question: can an AI reduce the exhausting parts of connection without reducing human choice?

My stance: the AI can listen, coach, filter, and explain. It cannot impersonate a partner, make the final decision, or quietly turn private insecurities into a product advantage. Consent, inspection, deletion, authenticity, and a clear no are core features.

The best version would help someone understand what they want, show up as themselves, and meet a real person with less noise around the connection. This is still the work before the launch. The hard part starts after people trust you with the truth about themselves. I am still proving I can build something worthy of that trust.

Why this question

How I work

A small standard for a consequential product

  • Start with the person Name the ordinary moment Who is asking, what do they need, and what would make the interaction worth returning to?
  • Make the system legible Disclosure belongs in the experience People should know what they are using, where an answer came from, and when a human can take over.
  • Design for the day after A handoff is part of the product The work is not finished when the page ships. Someone still has to update it, answer for it, and improve it.

The work around the idea

Research, product, and the handoff between people

One sharp rule keeps me honest: give the person more agency than the system takes. The concierge idea is the center of gravity here. The shipped websites and classroom work are where I learn whether a product can survive contact with people, constraints, and the handoff after I leave. Each room has to earn its place.

I publish limits before claims because engagement is not a vanity number. It is whether someone understands what the system knows, feels safe correcting it, and chooses to come back.
